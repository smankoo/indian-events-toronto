01 · The problemA feed of flyers nobody needed

Today the account posts one generic image for nearly every listing it finds on one website. A Falguni Pathak concert looks the same as a trivia night, big announcements arrive late or not at all, and there's no reason to follow rather than check Sulekha yourself.

The accounts that repost every flyer stay small; the ones people follow do the choosing for them. In Toronto, the desi scene is all promoters posting their own nights. Nobody is curating it. That's the gap.

02 · Who it's forFour kinds of follower

The fan

"Tell me the second Diljit announces."

Follows artists. Wants alerts, on-sale dates and a heads-up before things sell out.

The weekend planner

"What are we doing Saturday?"

Twenties and thirties, downtown and the 905. Bollywood nights, comedy, qawwali, the good parties.

The family organiser

"Where do we take Mom and the kids for Diwali?"

Festivals, melas, classical and devotional evenings, things that start before 9 PM.

The community regular

"Is there garba near me this week?"

Garba people, Punjabi music, Tamil stand-up, ghazal nights. Wants their niche, complete.

05 · Editorial lineWhat we leave out is the brand

We post

  • Desi events in and around the GTA that we'd tell a friend about
  • Big names the moment they're announced
  • Every festival celebration, big and small, in one guide
  • Community nights inside the roundups and guides

We don't

  • Repost every flyer as its own post
  • Singles mixers, networking nights, deal hunting
  • Promoter superlatives we can't check ("Canada's biggest…")
  • Invented faces. AI only cleans up a real photo of the real artist
  • Paid placement without a clear "Sponsored" label

Voice: yes

"Live qawwali and dinner at Matka Biryani House"

"$10 Tamil stand-up at a Mississauga physio clinic"

"His first Toronto show since 2022"

Voice: no

"🔥🔥 THE BIGGEST BOLLYWOOD PARTY OF THE YEAR!!! 🔥🔥"

"Don't miss this unforgettable night of epic vibes"

Specific, warm, plain, a little wry. Facts from the listing only.

06 · How it runsThe machine drafts, you decide in 10 minutes

FindSulekha, Eventbrite, AllEvents and Ticketmaster, twice a day, plus a promoter submission form later
UnderstandWho's headlining, what kind of night, which community, how big, which festival
DraftProposed posts with images, captions and "why go" lines, ready to approve
You decideOne tap per post on your phone. Alerts same day; the weekly batch takes about 10 minutes

Every correction you make is remembered: similar events come with your past call attached, repeated corrections become rules you confirm once, and the "how big is this" scoring is retuned to your actual choices. Formats we consistently agree on can eventually be handled automatically, with a sample still sent for review.

Example from building this page: the drafts called a party "Canada's longest running Bollywood party" and placed a comedy night in Scarborough. Both took seconds to fix, and that's exactly the job of the review.

08 · Getting thereRun it by hand first, then automate

Weeks 1–2Now

Run this exact format by hand: I draft each post, you approve, we publish. Starts with this weekend's roundup and the Jubin Nautiyal alert. Needs Instagram/Facebook access.

Weeks 3–6

Automate the drafting for weekends, features and guides; a phone-friendly review queue with one-tap decisions; link-in-bio becomes a filterable calendar.

Weeks 6–10

Ticketmaster and change tracking for alerts (announced, on sale, second show, sold out); a promoter submission form; a festival calendar so guides go out on time.

Later

A weekly email or WhatsApp channel people can own; recap Reels with promoters; clearly labelled sponsored slots if and when it's worth it.
